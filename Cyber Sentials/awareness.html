01 // MAGAZINE PROLOGUE

The architecture of modern deception.

Cybercrime is rarely about brute-forcing software passwords. It is psychological social engineering—manipulating human emotion to bypass digital locks from within.

PRINCIPIUM 01

What Cybercrime Is

Any criminal offense conducted through digital infrastructure—smartphones, internet banking, or payment networks. It ranges from unauthorized UPI siphoning to deepfake impersonation and extortion.

PRINCIPIUM 02

Why Fraud is Exploding

Real-time payment rails like UPI and frictionless mobile apps allow stolen money to disperse through multiple "mule" accounts within seconds, complicating traditional police recovery timelines.

PRINCIPIUM 03

Urgency, Fear & Trust

Scammers manufacture psychological emergencies: an impending electric disconnection, an intercepted narcotics parcel, or an expiring job offer. Panic disables logic and drives rushed actions.

PRINCIPIUM 04

Awareness as First Defense

No banking encryption can save an account if a user voluntarily yields an OTP or scans a reverse QR code. Informed skepticism is the citizen’s single most impenetrable firewall.

02 // INVESTIGATIVE DOSSIERS

Case studies: The 6 major Indian fraud vectors.

Examine in-depth breakdowns of real-world scam patterns reported across state cyber crime cells and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.

DOSSIER 01 CREDENTIAL THEFT
Visual Evidence // Vector 01 Fake telecom and banking portals lure victims into volunteering personal 6-digit OTPs under false threats of SIM deactivation.
TELECOM & KYC IMPERSONATION

1. Jamtara-Style Phishing & Vishing

Phone calls and deceptive SMS messages impersonating bank customer care, electricity boards, or telecom KYC desks to extract banking credentials, card numbers, and OTPs.

Mechanism

Callers claim your bank account or SIM card will be deactivated within 24 hours. They send a shortened link leading to a cloned banking login page or ask you to verbally disclose an OTP received on your device.

Warning Signs

  • Calls originating from ordinary 10-digit personal numbers
  • Extreme urgency threatening immediate account deactivation
  • Solicitation of full card CVV numbers, expiry dates, or SMS OTPs

Protection Rule

Banks NEVER solicit passwords, PINs, or OTPs over phone calls. Verify alerts solely by calling the phone number printed on the back of your bank debit card.

If Affected

Instantly block your debit card via net banking. Call your bank's 24/7 fraud helpline and report the transaction reference number to helpline 1930.

Editorial Rule: An SMS OTP is a master authorization key. Never read it out to anyone over a phone call.
DOSSIER 02 VIDEO EXTORTION
Visual Evidence // Vector 02 Fraudsters stage artificial police station backdrops and wear replica uniforms over Skype/WhatsApp video calls to terrify victims.
AUTHORITY INTIMIDATION

2. Digital Arrest & Video Extortion

Criminals masquerading as police officers, Customs, CBI, or ED personnel conduct video calls claiming a seized courier with narcotics or money laundering bears the victim’s Aadhaar.

Mechanism

The victim is told an illegal courier was booked in their name. The call is transferred to a fake police officer on Skype who demands they stay on video call under "digital arrest", threatening imprisonment unless funds are sent to an "escrow account".

Warning Signs

  • Insistence on continuous video connection without disconnecting
  • Displaying forged arrest warrants, FIR copies, or Supreme Court letters
  • Demands to transfer savings into "RBI verification accounts"

Protection Rule

Legal Reality: Indian law does not possess any concept of "Digital Arrest". Real police never conduct interrogations or demand fund transfers over video calls.

If Affected

Hang up immediately. Block the number. Report the caller's mobile details to sancharsaathi.gov.in (Chakshu) and register an FIR on cybercrime.gov.in.

Statutory Law: State Police and Central Agencies always issue formal physical summons under Section 41A CrPC / relevant BNSS provisions.
DOSSIER 03 PAYMENT MANIPULATION
Visual Evidence // Vector 03 Scammers exploit user ignorance of UPI mechanics by sending payment authorization QR codes falsely labeled as "Receive Money".
MARKETPLACE FRAUD

3. UPI Collect Requests & QR Code Scams

Tricking online sellers on OLX or marketplace apps into scanning QR codes or approving collect requests under the false belief that doing so deposits money into their bank account.

Mechanism

When selling items online, a prospective buyer agrees to pay in advance. They send a QR code or initiate a UPI collect request, instructing the seller to scan it and enter their PIN to "claim" the transferred funds.

Warning Signs

  • Buyer claiming corporate accounts require sellers to scan codes
  • UPI screens showing a prominent "PAY" button with an amount
  • Any instruction demanding your secret 4-digit or 6-digit PIN

Protection Rule

The Golden UPI Rule: Entering a UPI PIN always authorizes money to LEAVE your account. Receiving payments or refunds NEVER requires a PIN or scanning a code.

If Affected

Immediately open your mobile banking app to block UPI services. Contact your bank to request an immediate lien hold and dial 1930 within 2 hours.

Core Fact: Receiving money via UPI requires only your registered mobile number or UPI ID—nothing more.
DOSSIER 04 FINANCIAL PONZI
Visual Evidence // Vector 04 Cloned investment apps display simulated, artificial portfolio gains to coax victims into depositing tens of lakhs of rupees.
SECURITIES FRAUD

4. Fake Investment & Trading Apps

Cloned trading platforms, fraudulent Telegram advisory groups, and fake cryptocurrency websites displaying fabricated gains that can never be withdrawn.

Mechanism

Victims are added to Telegram groups sharing forged profit screenshots. They are encouraged to deposit small sums, which display inflated artificial gains. When attempting to withdraw, scammers demand "tax clearance fees" or "forex release deposits", draining further capital.

Warning Signs

  • Promises of guaranteed returns (e.g. 20% to 50% weekly profit)
  • Instructing investments to be sent to personal savings accounts via UPI
  • Apps downloaded from third-party APK links rather than official app stores

Protection Rule

Only invest through brokers registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Verify SEBI registration numbers on official portals before transferring money.

If Affected

Cease sending any additional "clearance fees". Save all chat records, transfer receipts, and beneficiary UPI IDs. Lodge a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in.

SEBI Statutory Notice: High financial returns with zero market risk do not exist. Fixed returns on stock equities are legally impossible.
DOSSIER 05 WORK-FROM-HOME TRAP
Visual Evidence // Vector 05 Fraudsters disburse small real payments (₹200 to ₹500) for simple rating tasks to build trust, before trapping victims in non-refundable deposit schemes.
EMPLOYMENT FRAUD

5. Job & Task-Based Pre-Paid Scams

Unsolicited WhatsApp or Telegram messages offering part-time work-from-home jobs such as rating hotels on Google Maps or liking YouTube videos for cash payouts.

Mechanism

In Phase 1, fraudsters disburse small genuine payments (₹200 to ₹500) for simple tasks to build trust. In Phase 2, the victim is added to a "VIP task" group requiring an upfront deposit (₹5,000 to ₹1,00,000) to unlock higher rewards, after which withdrawals are permanently frozen.

Warning Signs

  • Messages from international country codes (+62, +84, +234) offering part-time roles
  • Requirement to pay money in order to work or unlock earned wages
  • Communication restricted strictly to anonymous Telegram group channels

Protection Rule

Legitimate employers never demand upfront security deposits or task fees to disburse salaries. Treat any job requiring an upfront deposit as an immediate scam.

If Affected

Capture screenshots of Telegram groups, bank account transfer details, and deposit slips. Notify your bank branch to initiate chargeback claims and file on 1930.

Psychological Insight: The small initial reward is psychological bait designed to eliminate skepticism before demanding major deposits.
DOSSIER 06 TELECOM INTERCEPTION
Visual Evidence // Vector 06 Attackers acquire duplicate SIM cards using leaked identity documents, leaving the victim’s phone without cellular network while intercepting two-factor banking SMS.
IDENTITY HIJACKING

6. SIM Swap & Telecom Interception

Criminals obtain a duplicate SIM card registered under your identity, causing your genuine SIM card to lose network coverage while they intercept banking OTPs.

Mechanism

Using stolen Aadhaar details and forged ID documents, fraudsters convince telecom operators that your SIM was lost. Once the duplicate SIM activates, your phone loses all network signal, allowing the attacker to receive your incoming OTPs and reset net banking passwords.

Warning Signs

  • Sudden, persistent loss of mobile network service in strong coverage areas
  • Repeated missed calls from strange numbers immediately before service drops
  • SMS alerts from your telecom operator confirming a SIM upgrade request

Protection Rule

If your phone abruptly displays "No Service" for more than 30 minutes, immediately call your telecom operator from another phone to check if a duplicate SIM was issued.

If Affected

Contact your mobile provider immediately to block the duplicate SIM. Notify your bank to freeze net banking and UPI access, and register the incident on 1930.

Sanchar Saathi Safety Tool: Use the government's TAFCOP portal on sancharsaathi.gov.in to check all mobile numbers registered under your identity document.

CASE 01 🇮🇳 INDIA Financial Fraud
Forensic Illustration // Staged Judicial Video Coercion
REPORTED MONETARY EXTORTION ₹11 Crore (~$1.32M USD) Reporting Period: January 2025 // Bengaluru, Karnataka
DOCS // 2025-IN-01

Bengaluru Digital Arrest Fraud: 11-Day Psychological Hostage Crisis

Reported Date: January 2025 • Target Profile: Senior Software Professional / Tech Executive

1. What Happened?

A senior software professional residing in Bengaluru was contacted by criminals claiming a courier consignment addressed in his name had been intercepted containing narcotics and illegal passports. Over an ordeal spanning 11 consecutive days, the perpetrators kept the victim locked in a continuous video interrogation, systematically coercing him into liquidating company equity, breaking fixed deposits, and transferring approximately ₹11 crore across designated bank accounts.

2. How Did the Attack Work?

The attackers combined multiple psychological coercion tactics:

  • Agency Escalation: Calls started from fake telecom and courier agents before being "transferred" to counterfeit police officers and the CBI.
  • Fabricated Judicial Ambience: Attackers staged a simulated Supreme Court hearing room on video, displaying court logos, formal robes, and fraudulent arrest warrants stamped with forged emblems.
  • Continuous Video Surveillance: The victim was ordered under threat of immediate physical SWAT raid to keep his webcam active 24/7, preventing any consultation with family, legal counsel, or colleagues.
  • Safe Holding Account Pretext: Funds were demanded under the ruse of "Supreme Court-mandated financial verification", falsely guaranteeing a full refund within 48 hours once his name was cleared.

3. What Made the Incident Serious?

This incident represents one of the single largest individual digital arrest extortions documented in Indian cybercrime history. It disproved the misconception that digital fraud targets only technologically naive or elderly demographics; a highly compensated IT professional was isolated, terrorized, and psychologically dominated through institutional intimidation and procedural mimicry.

💡 What Can Ordinary Internet Users Learn?

The Golden Legal Principle: Indian Police, the CBI, the Enforcement Directorate (ED), and the Supreme Court of India never arrest citizens over Skype, WhatsApp, or Google Meet video calls. "Digital arrest" is an entirely fabricated concept with zero legal existence in the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC / BNSS).

If any caller threatens arrest over video and demands financial transfers to "verify clean funds", disconnect immediately and dial the National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930.

CASE 02 🇮🇳 INDIA Financial Fraud
Forensic Illustration // Mule Account Laundering Funnel
OFFICIAL ENFORCEMENT ACTION ₹33 Lakh Siphoned & Layered Press Release Date: 27.01.2025 // Chennai, Tamil Nadu
DOCS // 2025-IN-02

Chennai Senior Citizen Extortion: Money Laundering Syndicate Arrests

Reported Date: January 2025 • Investigating Agency: Directorate of Enforcement (ED)

1. What Happened?

In January 2025, the Enforcement Directorate (ED) formally issued a press release detailing enforcement operations and multiple arrests in connection with an organized digital arrest fraud that extracted ₹33 lakh from an elderly Chennai resident. Scammers contacted the senior citizen accusing them of involvement in serious cross-border financial offenses, forcing compliance under threats of immediate imprisonment.

2. How Did the Attack Work?

The syndicates leveraged psychological isolation coupled with a sophisticated financial funnel:

  • Elder Vulnerability Exploitation: Scammers targeted an elderly citizen who lived independently, capitalizing on lack of familiarity with modern digital policing norms and fear of societal dishonor.
  • Fabricated Federal Allegations: Victims were told their identity credentials had surfaced in serious anti-money laundering and terror financing registries.
  • Immediate Layering via Mule Accounts: As soon as the victim transferred the ₹33 lakh to "secure verification accounts", the syndicate moved the funds within minutes through a tiered network of domestic bank accounts (mule accounts) and converted portions into cryptocurrency to evade bank freezes.

3. What Made the Incident Serious?

This case highlighted the direct nexus between digital arrest extortion and organized cross-border money laundering syndicates. It triggered formal prosecution under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), demonstrating that the actors behind digital arrests are rarely individual pranksters, but highly coordinated syndicates utilizing corporate-style mule account networks.

💡 What Can Ordinary Internet Users Learn?

Protect Senior Family Members: Elderly citizens are prime targets for fear-driven extortion schemes. Families must proactively discuss digital fraud tactics, install caller verification tools, and establish a family consultation rule before executing any substantial bank transfer.

Never allow anyone claiming to be a police or customs official to order you into silence. Legitimate law-enforcement summons are delivered through signed physical notices, never secret phone threats.

CASE 03 🇮🇳 INDIA Financial Fraud
Forensic Illustration // 42-Transaction Relentless Extortion Spiral
SYSTEMIC PSYCHOLOGICAL DOMINATION ₹7.67 Crore Extorted 42 Times Reporting Period: April 2025 // Rajasthan / CBI Operation Chakra
DOCS // 2025-IN-03

Rajasthan Digital Arrest: 3 Months of Intimidation and 42 Extortion Demands

Reported Date: April 2025 • Law Enforcement Agency: Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

1. What Happened?

In a prolonged case broken by national media in April 2025, a victim in Rajasthan was subjected to relentless digital arrest intimidation spanning three straight months. Fraudsters posing as federal agents manipulated the victim into making 42 distinct financial transactions, gradually siphoning an astronomical total of ₹7.67 crore before the victim finally broke isolation and sought help.

2. How Did the Attack Work?

The attackers maintained prolonged psychological dominance through iterative micro-extortions:

  • Sunk Cost Entrapment: After extracting the first few lakhs, scammers promised each subsequent transfer was the "final clearance fee" needed to close the investigation and refund all previously deposited funds.
  • Threat of Imprisonment for Non-Compliance: Whenever the victim hesitated, scammers staged sudden aggressive video alerts showing sirens, fake arrest teams, and court orders citing the Official Secrets Act.
  • Multi-City Kingpin Operations: Investigations revealed an organized network spanning Mumbai, Moradabad, and Rajasthan, coordinating live bank cash-outs and crypto conversions across borders.

3. What Made the Incident Serious?

This case exposed the devastating power of prolonged psychological conditioning. Cybercriminals do not simply steal money and disappear; in high-value cases, they maintain parasitic control over their victims for months. The incident prompted major interventions under CBI Operation Chakra, targeting interstate kingpins and dismantle organized digital arrest hubs.

💡 What Can Ordinary Internet Users Learn?

The Golden First-Hour Rule (The Golden Hour): Once you transfer money under coercion, every delay makes recovery significantly harder. Scammers never return "security deposits". The moment an official demands money to dismiss charges, recognize it as extortion.

Immediate reporting within the first 2–4 hours to 1930 allows the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) to trigger banking API freezes and stop funds in intermediary mule accounts.

CASE 04 🌐 INTERNATIONAL Cryptocurrency Theft
Forensic Illustration // Cold Wallet Key Injection & Exfiltration
RECORD BREAKING CRYPTO THEFT ~$1.5 Billion USD in ETH Incident Date: February 2025 // Global Digital Asset Market
DOCS // 2025-INTL-01

Bybit Cryptocurrency Theft: The Largest Digital Asset Heist in History

Reported Date: February 2025 • Industry Sector: Global Digital Asset Exchange / Web3 Infrastructure

1. What Happened?

In February 2025, cryptocurrency exchange Bybit suffered what is documented as the largest single cryptocurrency theft in history. Advanced threat actors penetrated internal key infrastructure and unauthorizedly transferred approximately $1.5 billion worth of Ethereum (ETH) out of the exchange's cold wallet architecture into hostile decentralized wallets.

2. How Did the Attack Work?

Security researchers and international cyber defense agencies documented the mechanism:

  • Compromise of Multi-Signature Infrastructure: Rather than attacking the underlying blockchain code, adversaries targeted off-chain administrative authentication and developer access mechanisms.
  • Rapid Obfuscation via Decentralized Protocols: The stolen digital assets were immediately fragmented across hundreds of intermediate wallets, smart contract mixers, and cross-chain bridges to prevent centralised blacklisting.
  • State-Sponsored APT Techniques: Analytical indicators observed by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) align with methodologies deployed by well-resourced state-backed cyber syndicates.

3. What Made the Incident Serious?

The Bybit heist surpassed previous record heists (including Ronin and Mt. Gox) in sheer dollar value. It revealed that even institutional digital asset custodians with multi-layered governance procedures remain vulnerable to credential theft, supply-chain infiltration, and multi-sig authorization exploitation.

💡 What Can Ordinary Internet Users Learn?

"Not Your Keys, Not Your Coins": Centralized cryptocurrency exchanges are custodial targets. Holding high-value personal crypto assets on an exchange exposes you to counterparty insolvency or exchange-level intrusion.

Ordinary investors should utilize personal self-custody hardware cold wallets (e.g. Ledger, Trezor), enforce hardware security key 2FA (YubiKey), and never store secret seed phrases digitally on cloud notes or desktop drives.

CASE 05 🌐 INTERNATIONAL Ransomware
Forensic Illustration // Healthcare Infrastructure Extortion & Disruption
CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE PARALYSIS ~190 Million Individuals Affected Incident: Feb 2024 / 2025 Comprehensive Review // United States
DOCS // 2025-INTL-02

Change Healthcare Ransomware: The Paralysis of National Medical Infrastructure

Reported Period: February 2024 / Comprehensive 2025 Audit • Threat Actor Group: ALPHV / BlackCat Ransomware Syndicate

1. What Happened?

In February 2024, Change Healthcare (a division of UnitedHealth Group processing over 15 billion healthcare billing transactions annually) was brought down by the ALPHV/BlackCat ransomware gang. The attack paralyzed electronic prescription processing across thousands of US pharmacies and exposed sensitive medical records of an estimated 190 million individuals—representing one of the most disruptive critical infrastructure attacks in history.

2. How Did the Attack Work?

Forensic audits confirmed an astonishingly simple point of failure:

  • Lack of Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Attackers used stolen credentials to remotely log into a critical Citrix gateway portal that did not have Multi-Factor Authentication enabled.
  • Double Extortion Deployment: Intruders spent days exfiltrating sensitive patient records and medical histories before deploying file encryption malware to lock down primary and backup transaction processing databases.
  • Ransom Payment & Re-Extortion: Despite a reported payment of approximately $22 million in Bitcoin, systemic recovery took months, costing billions in operational remediation.

3. What Made the Incident Serious?

This incident proved that cybersecurity is a matter of life and safety, not just IT finance. Patients could not obtain life-saving insulin or cancer medications at pharmacies because real-time insurance clearing was offline. Small medical practices faced immediate cash-flow collapse when claim processing stalled for weeks.

💡 What Can Ordinary Internet Users Learn?

Universal Rule: Never Skip Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): A multibillion-dollar healthcare backbone was breached through a single remote portal lacking 2FA. For personal accounts, always activate Multi-Factor Authentication—preferably using authenticator apps or hardware security keys rather than SMS OTPs.

Maintain offline copies of essential personal health records and maintain emergency financial cushions for occasions when electronic payment rails experience unexpected downtime.

03 // BEHAVIORAL SEQUENCE

The scam anatomy: Step-by-step killchain.

Almost every cyber fraud follows this predictable 5-stage progression. Click each stage below to examine how attackers guide their targets toward unauthorized transactions.

01 STAGE I
Trust
False legitimacy via spoofed identities, fake IDs, or uniforms.
02 STAGE II
Urgency
Manufactured panic: impending arrest, disconnection, or fees.
03 STAGE III
Isolation
Enforced secrecy preventing consultation with family or banks.
04 STAGE IV
Theft
QR scan, OTP forward, or remote access installation requested.
05 STAGE V
Loss
Rapid fund transfer into mule network; scammer disconnects.

Stage 1: Establishing False Trust

Scammers begin by manufacturing an appearance of legitimacy. They pose as bank officers, courier tracking agents (India Post/BlueDart), electricity officials, or even police personnel in uniform over video. They use forged ID cards, official logos, and caller ID spoofing to drop your psychological guard.
